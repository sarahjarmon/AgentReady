I Spent 6 Hours Debugging One Problem — And It Showed Me Why Small Businesses Need to Be Ready for AI Agents

AI agents are changing how customers discover, understand, and interact with businesses. Here’s what building Apt4AI is teaching me about AI readiness for small businesses — and why running an AI website audit is only the beginning.

Today, I spent six hours debugging one single issue.

Six hours.

Was it frustrating? Absolutely.

But somewhere between testing, fixing, breaking things, testing again, and wondering why something that should work still didn’t work, I realized something important:

This is exactly why I’m building Apt4AI.

Not because small business owners need another dashboard.

Not because they need another score.

And definitely not because they need another report telling them they have 17 things to fix on their website.

They need the opposite.

They need AI readiness to become simple.

Running an AI website audit is the easy part

There are already powerful AI tools that can analyze a website.

Give ChatGPT or Claude a website and the right context, and they can tell you a lot about it.

They can identify missing information, unclear positioning, weak content, confusing structure, and other potential problems.

So I keep asking myself:

Why would the world need another AI website audit tool?

It doesn’t.

At least, not if the product stops at the audit.

The real problem begins after you discover what’s wrong.

What happens after your AI readiness audit?

Imagine you run a small business.

You have a website.

You spent time and money building it.

Maybe it performs reasonably well on Google. Maybe customers already know your brand. Maybe you receive leads through search, social media, referrals, or advertising.

But the way people find businesses is changing.

Customers can increasingly ask AI systems and AI agents questions instead of manually searching through pages of websites.

They may ask:

  • “Find a business that can do this for me.”
  • “Which company offers this service?”
  • “How much does it cost?”
  • “Can I buy or book it online?”

For your business to participate effectively in that environment, an AI system needs to be able to find your business, understand what you offer, and determine how someone can take the next step.

That’s what I mean by AI readiness.

And if an audit discovers that an AI agent cannot clearly understand your offer, pricing, services, location, or conversion path, a score alone doesn’t solve anything.

It simply gives the problem back to you.

Small business owners shouldn’t have to become AI experts

This is the part I keep coming back to while building Apt4AI.

A typical diagnostic process looks something like this:

Find the problem → Read the report → Understand the technical recommendation → Figure out what to change → Modify the website → Test it → Check whether it worked → Monitor it over time.

For someone working in technology, that may sound manageable.

For a small business owner?

That’s another job.

And they already have one.

They have customers to serve, invoices to send, employees to manage, products to sell, appointments to handle, marketing to do, and a business to run.

They shouldn’t have to understand the technical mechanics of AI visibility, AI agents, structured website information, machine-readable content, or AI readiness just to remain discoverable.

That realization has changed what I want Apt4AI to become.

The hardest part isn’t finding the problem. It’s fixing it.

My original question was:

Can AI find, understand, and buy from this business?

That led to the three core areas Apt4AI examines:

Visibility

Can AI systems discover the business and access the information they need?

Understanding

Can they clearly determine what the business does, what it offers, who it serves, and the information necessary to describe it accurately?

Buyability

Can they determine what action a potential customer should take next?

Those questions still matter.

But increasingly, I’m convinced that the most important part comes afterward.

I don’t want Apt4AI to say:

“Here are your problems. Good luck fixing them.”

I want the experience to become:

FIND → FIX IT → VERIFY → MONITOR

And eventually:

One click.

What “Fix It” means for Apt4AI

This is the part I’m working toward.

Apt4AI identifies an AI-readiness issue.

Instead of simply explaining the problem, the goal is for Apt4AI to help apply the appropriate correction.

Then it checks again.

Did the fix actually work?

Can AI systems now access the information?

Can they understand it more clearly?

Can they identify what the business sells and what action a customer should take?

If the answer is yes, Apt4AI verifies the improvement.

Then monitoring begins, because websites change and the AI ecosystem changes with them.

That creates a very different product from a one-time website audit.

It becomes a continuous process:

Find. Fix. Verify. Monitor.

Why this is much harder to build

From the user’s perspective, I want this to feel ridiculously simple.

Enter your website.

See what matters.

Fix it.

That’s precisely what makes the technology behind it difficult.

A diagnostic system only needs to identify a problem.

An action system has to understand the problem, determine an appropriate correction, apply or guide that correction safely, verify the result, handle failures, and avoid damaging something that was already working.

Today, one piece of that process cost me six hours.

And yes, I was frustrated.

But it also confirmed something for me:

I’m working on the right problem.

Because if this is complicated for someone spending her days building the system, imagine telling every small business owner that they need to figure it out themselves.

That’s not the solution.

Google-ready was once enough. AI-ready is becoming another layer.

For years, businesses learned that having a website wasn’t enough.

They had to think about search engines.

SEO became part of running a business online because being on the internet didn’t automatically mean customers could find you.

Now another interface is developing between businesses and customers: AI assistants and AI agents.

That doesn’t mean Google or traditional SEO suddenly disappears.

It means businesses increasingly have another question to consider:

When an AI system is looking for a business like mine, what does it understand about me?

Does it know what I sell?

Does it understand who I serve?

Can it find accurate information?

Can it determine the next action?

Those are the questions I believe businesses will increasingly need to answer.
